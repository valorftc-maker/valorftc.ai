The method

most of the work is the machinery, not the signal

Most of the work in quant trading is not finding a signal. It is building machinery honest enough to tell you the signal isn't real — and then believing it.

Every idea runs the same gauntlet. What died is kept: the archive and the trials ledger are how the deflated Sharpe stays honest.

Costs, four ways. Single research backtests (the desk funnel's backtest records) charge the per-class model in /api/state.costs: equity 4 bp per turn (2 spread + 1 commission + 1 slippage, 50 bp/yr borrow on shorts); crypto 35 bp per turn (5 + 25 + 5). The SVT sweep charges per side, tiered by each name's liquidity (src/tqd/webapp/svt_engine.py cost_per_side: 2 bp per side at median daily dollar volume >= $50M; 10 bp per side at median daily dollar volume >= $5M; 30 bp below; crypto sides from the same config) — in the run's own words, "costs come from config/tqd.yaml - liquidity-tiered 2-30bp equity sides, 32.5bp crypto sides (venue-true) - with borrow on shorts". Paper fills on the desk book pay a flat 10 bp per fill (manage.py FILL_COST). Venue fills are booked at the venue's fill with the venue's fee schedule applied by execution.py fill_net (the crypto fee is taken in the asset at Alpaca; stocks commission-free).

Risk & governance

read from /api/state and /api/broker at publish

The research engine's mode is paper (paper; block_live() raised, as it must). The research engine never trades; the desk's execution layer, below, is what is armed. A person arms live trading with a typed acknowledgement that lapses after 24 hours. Once armed, the desk's management loop and the desk's own buttons route orders to the venue, and the book records only what the venue actually filled — its price, its size, its fee (policy). The assistant and the tower's agents never hold keys and never place orders (policy).
